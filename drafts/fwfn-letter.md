Dear Ms. Pervais,

I volunteer with OpenStreetMap, the free, community-built map of the world.
Its data is used by many apps and services, including navigation, delivery
routing and some emergency-response tools. I am preparing to add the civic
addresses that are missing from OpenStreetMap in Thunder Bay, using the address
points the City of Thunder Bay publishes as open data.

The City's address layer also includes 378 address points on Fort William First
Nation land: on Squaw Bay Road, Mission Road, City Road, Mountain Road, Anemki
Drive, Chippewa Road and others. The City publishes them under its open data
licence. I did not want to add addresses on your Nation's land without asking
first, so I am writing before doing anything.

I would be grateful for your guidance on five things:

1. **Is Fort William First Nation comfortable with these addresses being added
   to OpenStreetMap?** If not, we will leave them out, and say so on the
   project's public page.
2. **Who assigns civic addresses on FWFN land: the Nation, or the City under
   an agreement?** I want to describe the source correctly.
3. **Does the Nation keep its own address records?** If so, and if you would
   be willing to share them, we would much rather use the Nation's own data
   than the City's copy.
4. **What is the current name of the road the City's data lists as "Squaw Bay
   Road"?** We will not add that name. We will use whatever name the Nation
   uses, or none until we hear from you.
5. **Is there a process or a person you would like us to go through,** or
   anything you would like done differently, such as a different set of
   addresses or different street spellings?

Nothing on FWFN land will be added until we have heard from you. If we have
not heard back within 30 days, we will leave these addresses out. Every
addition is reviewed by a person and can be traced back to its source. The full plan is public at {wiki link}.

Thank you for your time. I am happy to talk by phone or meet if that is easier.

Kind regards,
Konstantin Koniev
toronto@comentality.com

## Notes for the operator (not part of the letter)

- **Wait: 30 days, decided 2026-11-08.** If there is no reply by then, the
  FWFN phase is skipped (wiki § Fort William First Nation).
- **What precedent there is** (research 2026-11-07):
  - No Canadian OSM import found that consulted a First Nation or left out
    reserve land.
  - [Import/Guidelines](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Import/Guidelines)
    asks permission only of the data owner, here the City.
  - The nearest written norm is Australian: "defer to that community"
    ([Australia's First Peoples](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Australian_Tagging_Guidelines/Australia%27s_First_Peoples)).
  - [OCAP](https://fnigc.ca/ocap-training/) (Ownership, Control, Access,
    Possession) is framed around research data. Whether it covers civic
    points a city has already published is unsettled, so treat it as the
    spirit to work in, not as a rule.
  - Counterpoint: an Alberta First Nation *asked* for its addresses to be in
    OSM ([forum](https://community.openstreetmap.org/t/use-of-amdsp-data-in-open-street-map/110187)).
- **Why the City has these points is unverified.** None of the 378 has a parcel
  number (`PCL_PIN`), and every City row does. That fits the City keeping them
  for emergency dispatch: reportedly Thunder Bay Fire Rescue answers calls in
  FWFN under an agreement (unverified). Question 2 is there to settle it.
- **8 of the 378 have no civic number** (3 on City Rd, 2 on Back St, 1 on
  Anemki Dr, 2 with no street). They can't become addresses and are dropped
  whatever FWFN says.
- **FWFN's land code:** a draft dated 2023-03-14 is posted on the
  [Land Management page](https://fwfn.com/departments/land-management/); not
  confirmed ratified. If the Nation points to it, read it before uploading.
